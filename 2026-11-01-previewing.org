Previews that look like they belong, appear almost instantly, never block Emacs, and update as you type

In [[file:2021-07-31-citations.org][July 2021]] I was thrilled to announce the long-awaited feature of citation
syntax. Today I get to announce a feature a bit closer to my heart, a dramatic
improvement to (actually a complete overhaul of) Org's system for generating LaTeX
previews!

In late 2021, Karthik and I discussed our mutual dissatisfaction with the status
quo, and in 2022 we embarked on a project to improve the experience by ripping out the
entire LaTeX preview implementation, and building something better in its place.
What started as a "brief" collaborative project has turned into a multi-year
slog with a long tail of annoying issues, but after exchanging a bit over ten
thousand messages and working on it for a few hundred hours, I think it's fair
to say we've succeeded.

Let me give you a tour of the new work; if you're interested in reading more of
the details, see Karthik's companion post [[https://karthinks.com/software/understanding-org-latex-preview/][Understanding Org's LaTeX preview
system]].
* The Status Quo

Many Org users are intimately familiar with LaTeX, and appreciate the close
integration of LaTeX snippets in the Org syntax. I was one of the many
mathematics students inspired by Gilles Castel's fantastic [[https://web.archive.org/web/20261020234323/https://castel.dev/post/lecture-notes-1/][blog post]]
demonstrating his system for quick LaTeX snippets, and my quest to replicate the
experience in Emacs and produce detailed notes led to my intimate acquaintance
with the particularities of Org's LaTeX preview system, namely:

- Every fragment is compiled individually in a fresh LaTeX process
- Fragments are compiled sequentially, and block until all are complete
- A file with =#+startup: latexpreview= will typically freeze Emacs for \(n(\text{fragments}) \times \frac{1}{4}\) seconds
- There is a very loose connection between in-buffer text size and preview size
- The baseline never matches
- Colours are fixed at generation time, and so a theme change requires
  clearing + regenerating all fragments
- LaTeX errors (like =\(1+1\foobar\)=) are silently hidden

Over the years other people have tried to improve the experience with config
band-aids and packages that try to work around aspects of the design (most
notably [[https://github.com/io12/org-fragtog][org-fragtog]] and [[https://github.com/dandavison/xenops][xenops]]). All of these approaches made severe
compromises. Karthik wasn't happy with the status quo, and started showing you
could make better compromises; I didn't like making compromises. So, we ended up
throwing out the old implementation and starting from scratch 💥.
* Shiny new features
** Fragmented (async) generation

For LaTeX fragments to feel like an /ambient/ part of the Org buffer, there are a
few crucial things we need to get right. One of the most important is making
sure that they never interrupt your interaction with the buffer.

It is not at all pleasant for Emacs to freeze when opening a buffer, or with a
package like =org-fragtog=, while typing. To prevent this from happening we've
split the rendering pipeline up and execute every stage of it asynchronously,
with bookkeeping to make sure we put the right overlay images in the right
place, even when the buffer's changed during fragment generation.

#+caption: The new fragment rendering pipeline
[[file:figures/olp-rendering-pipeline.svg]]

Karthik and I have done an extensive amount of profiling and tuning to minimise
pauses during the pipeline to make sure you never feel Emacs hitch during
preview generation.
** Fast fragments

Now, while it's nice for fragment previews to no longer hold up interacting with
your Org buffer, it's also nice to see them quickly. We now go to great lengths
to minimise the time it takes to see previews placed in the buffer. Details
aside, a moderately mathematical document holding 660 fragments that used to
take two and a half /minutes/ to load now takes two /seconds/. In fact, the
difference is even more pronounced as we order fragment generation based on the
current buffer position, so you see nearby fragments stream in first.

This new pipeline is built using =org-async=, a small declarative process-chaining
library introduced as part of the patch set.

We also make use of =org-persist= to cache fragments, which means that this \sim{}70\times
performance improvement is /the worst case/. The fragments in a re-opened buffer
should appear near-instantly (see ~org-latex-preview-cache~ and adjust
~org-latex-preview-persist-expiry~ to change how long old previews are kept for).
** Fitting in with a splash of colour

A particular pet peeve of mine has been that previewed fragments never quite
looked at home in the Org buffer. The sizing was always a bit off, the
positioning not quite right. We now collect the sizing information needed to
harmonise the size of fragments' images with the buffer's text, producing
perfect baseline alignment and matched sizing (depending on your LaTeX/Emacs
font pairing, a minor correction may be needed; see
~org-latex-preview-appearance-options~).

If you're wondering how much work this took to calibrate, just know that there's
an unexplained 1.659393% correction factor needed to align the calculated and
observed sizing.

These fragments also follow the text scale and the current theme's foreground
colour, helping them feel like part of the buffer's text.
** Live updating too!

As part of the reworked preview engine, we wanted to subsume the functionality
of =org-fragtog=, a package for intelligently toggling previews as you move into
and out of them. This is provided with a new minor mode, ~org-latex-preview-mode~.

Thanks to the speed of the new pipeline, we realised that we could not just
re-render an edited fragment rapidly, but re-render it /during/ editing. When
~org-latex-preview-mode-display-live~ is non-nil, the preview mode also shows
live-updating previews.

This can be particularly helpful for catching mistakes early, since we now also
indicate errors by applying the =error= face and putting the compilation log for
the fragment in a tooltip.
** Nicely numbered

Previously, every numbered equation was compiled in isolation, and so rendered
as (1). With the work we've done on tracking fragments across the buffer, I
couldn't resist the temptation to make equation numbering actually correspond
to equation order.
** Equitable exports

The improvements in preview generation speed and sizing have been carried
through to the export backends. In particular, HTML and ODT (via a new =ox-mathml=
library) exports get the same numbered, aligned, and coloured images.
** Caveats

While I said earlier that I didn't want to make any compromises in the preview
experience, unfortunately it hasn't been possible to enable that experience in
all configurations. In particular:
- Live previews need =\\(...\\)= / =\\[...\\]=, since =$= is ambiguous while typing
- XeLaTeX and LuaLaTeX can't use precompiled preambles, so they're noticeably slower than pdfLaTeX
* Getting it early

This overhaul will be part of the Org 10.0 release. Until then, you can get the
shiny new goodness from the =main= branch (and help iron out the long tail of
bugs!). If you've been enjoying =org-fragtog=, you can drop it in favour of the
new =org-latex-preview-mode=.

For the best experience, make sure you have =mylatexformat.sty= and =preview.sty=
installed as part of your LaTeX distribution, along with =dvisvgm= 3.1+ or =dvipng=.

In the future, you might even see this work be reused to bring previews to other
modes like ~calc~ 😉
